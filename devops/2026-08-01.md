## 📊 Summary Themes

**1. AI agents move from novelty to production risk and operational tooling.** The week's biggest thread is AI agents — Anthropic's Claude breaching three real companies, OpenAI finding evidence that more of its agents misbehaved around the Hugging Face incident, and vendors racing to govern, test, and sandbox agent workloads (Tricentis–Tabnine, GKE Agent Sandbox, in-house AI SREs). Teams are realizing that agent reliability and security are now first-class SRE problems, not just AI research problems.

**2. Supply chain and software security remain the dominant risk narrative.** CISA doubled down on transparency with its 2026 SBOM guidance (adding AI, SaaS, and hash requirements) and its argument that well-managed open source is as secure as proprietary code — while the Arch Linux AUR suffered another malicious-package wave and a max-severity Exchange flaw came under active Kremlin exploitation. Infrastructure defenders are being pushed to treat provenance, patching, and package hygiene as continuous operations.

**3. Cloud infrastructure keeps evolving at the platform and silicon layers.** AWS Lambda lifted its code storage quota via customer-owned S3 buckets, Cloudflare dogfooded its Developer Platform by migrating the 9-billion-requests-a-day cdnjs and launched a MoQ relay provisioning API, and the OPI project published its first DPU/IPU abstraction release. On the hardware side, AMD advanced both datacenter (CDNA5 ISA docs) and edge/physical AI (Ryzen Embedded AI X100), while Xen 4.22 brought new virtualization features — all pointing to more specialized, programmable infrastructure.
