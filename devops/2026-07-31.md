## 📊 Summary Themes

**1. AI-Native Infrastructure and Agentic DevOps Matures**
This week's news is dominated by the convergence of AI agents and infrastructure tooling. GitHub's Copilot code review agents reaching GA, AWS Lambda MicroVMs enabling safer AI sandboxes, OpenAI open-sourcing Codex Security CLI, and Tricentis acquiring Tabnine for AI testing all point to an industry shifting from "AI-assisted coding" to "AI-driven operations." SRE teams are increasingly expected to integrate AI agents directly into CI/CD pipelines, code review workflows, and even infrastructure deployment decisions.

**2. Cloud Hyperscalers Race on Compute, Resiliency, and Express Deployments**
AWS launched Graviton5 C9g instances, CloudFormation Express mode (4x faster deploys), EKS rollback support, and Lambda MicroVMs — all in the past week. Azure responded with its GPT-5.6 Foundry integration and a deep dive on AI-driven resiliency engineering ("Brain"). Cloudflare continued its platform expansion with Internal DNS GA, Cache Response Rules, and post-quantum authentication. The hyperscalers are sprinting on three fronts: faster/better compute silicon, AI integration, and operational resilience.

**3. Infrastructure Governance Under Pressure from AI, Regulation, and Open-Source Policy**
Three distinct governance stories emerged: the GCC steering committee explicitly rejecting AI-generated code contributions (except tests), a Philadelphia suburb imposing 43 conditions on data center construction amid EPA deregulation debates, and Cloudflare's Q2 disruption report documenting how natural disasters and government actions continue to threaten internet reliability. Together, these highlight that as AI accelerates infrastructure buildout, governance — both technical (compiler policy, code review standards) and regulatory (data center zoning, net resilience) — is struggling to keep pace.
