## 📊 Summary Themes

**1. Agentic AI Enters Production — With Growing Pains** — The defining story of this week is the maturation of AI coding agents from experimental tools to production-deployed workforce participants. Meta launched its own agent, AWS added agentic workspaces to Kiro, and Microsoft rolled out token budgets for Copilot, signaling that the era of unlimited, unmeasured AI usage is ending. But reliability guardrails, SLO framework adaptations, and production-access risks highlight that organizations are still grappling with how to safely integrate autonomous AI into engineering workflows. The question has shifted from "Can we deploy AI agents?" to "How do we govern them at scale?"

**2. Supply-Chain Security Under Siege** — Two devastating stories dominated this week: the Shai-Hulud ChainDrop worm infecting 400+ npm packages with 2 billion monthly downloads, and GitHub extending malware advisories beyond npm to unify cross-ecosystem threat intelligence. Combined with CISA's updated SBOM guidance now covering AI model components and hardware-layer vulnerabilities threatening thousands of servers, the picture is clear: software supply chains are under coordinated attack from multiple vectors, and defense requires broader visibility, faster detection, and governance that spans code, dependencies, models, and even firmware.

**3. Kubernetes Evolves for the AI Era** — Kubernetes is being reshaped by AI demands in fundamental ways. DRA (Dynamic Resource Allocation) in K8s 1.34 solves the longstanding GPU scheduling problem, enabling multitenant AI inference with near-optimal utilization. Meanwhile, thought leadership from projects like kagent challenges the conventional Pod-per-agent pattern, pushing teams toward more efficient workload models. As Microsoft routes AI agent traffic across AKS and HashiCorp ships Vault Kubernetes key management, K8s is cementing its role as the de facto operating system not just for containers, but for the entire AI infrastructure stack.
